Who I am

I'm an engineering student who builds things. I'm entrepreneurial by temperament and philosophical by habit, and I like problems where technology meets a real opportunity. My long-term direction is energy and space systems, ideally with a venture-building angle rather than a purely corporate path.

How I got here

I didn't start with AI. I tried game development, web design and JavaScript, and graphic design, including digital Arabic calligraphy. Each taught me something, but none held me the way building AI tools did, so that's where I settled.

How I work

I build through AI-assisted development: I research a problem thoroughly, direct AI tools to implement it, then test, break and refine the result. My hand-written Python is basic, and I'd rather say so plainly than pretend otherwise. What I bring is choosing problems worth solving, designing the system, and shipping something that actually runs. I have working knowledge of machine-learning basics such as linear and logistic regression, and I'm studying model architectures in more depth.

What I've built

An offline runner for Microsoft's BitNet models, an exoplanet classifier for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2025, a multi-tokenizer token counter, and a database-backed web game. I've also experimented with quantized local models and retrieval-augmented generation. Details are on the home page.

What I'm exploring

Space and power: I'm looking into degradation-aware adaptive MPPT for CubeSat solar arrays, which is early-stage work, and I'm sketching ideas for NASA Space Apps 2026. On the AI side, I'm learning how architectures like JEPA work well enough to argue about them.

Looking for

Internships and early engineering experience, student competitions, open-source collaboration, and conversations with people building in energy, space or AI. Get in touch.
